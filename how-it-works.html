How Redcall works, step by step

Redcall has two sides: what a staff member does during an alert, and what a manager does to coordinate the response. Here's the full flow for each.

For staff

  1. 1

    Sign in at the start of a shift

    Staff sign in on their own phone when they start work, so Redcall knows who is on site for that shift.

  2. 2

    Raise an alert

    If something happens, a staff member taps the alert button and picks the type of situation from a short list, such as medical, security, fire, or other.

  3. 3

    Respond to an alert

    Other on-site staff get a notification with guidance written for that situation. Each person marks themselves safe or not safe, and can add a short note.

  4. 4

    Update if the situation changes

    Staff can change their status at any point while the alert is active, so the manager's view stays current.

For managers

  1. 1

    See the response as it happens

    When an alert is raised, the manager gets a single view of who has marked safe, who has marked not safe, and who hasn't responded yet.

  2. 2

    Follow up directly

    The manager can contact anyone who hasn't responded using their usual contact method, and decide whether to escalate the situation, including calling 000.

  3. 3

    Close the alert

    Once the situation is resolved, the manager ends the alert. Everyone is notified it's over, and a summary is kept on record for reporting.

How presence works

Redcall focuses on who's on site, based on staff signing in at the start of a shift and on how they respond when an alert is raised. It is not a location-tracking system and does not use GPS to pinpoint where someone is on the property.

If a staff member doesn't sign in, or doesn't respond to an alert, Redcall can't confirm their status automatically. Presence is only as accurate as the last time someone checked in, and it depends on staff keeping their own status up to date.

Practising the process with drill mode

Drill mode lets a manager run a practice alert that is clearly marked as a drill on every staff member's screen, so the team can rehearse the trigger, response, and coordination flow without it being treated as a real incident.

This is useful for onboarding new staff and for the regular emergency preparedness drills many properties already run.
