What Redcall does today

Every feature below is live in the app now. Where software has a real limit, we say so further down this page.

Alerting

  • A staff member raises an alert from their own phone in one tap, with no extra hardware to carry or charge.

  • Each alert is tagged with a situation type, such as medical, security, fire, or other, and carries guidance written for that situation.

  • Every staff member signed in for that shift is notified at the same time.

Coordination

  • A live response view shows who has marked safe, who has marked not safe, and who hasn't responded.

  • Staff can attach a short note when they respond, so the manager sees more than a status.

  • Staff can change their status at any point while the alert stays active.

Management

  • A manager runs the whole alert from a single screen, from the moment it's raised to closing it out.

  • The response view makes it clear who still needs following up, so the manager knows who to contact directly.

  • A summary of each alert, including response times and status, is kept on record for reporting after the event.

  • Drill mode runs a practice alert that's clearly marked as a drill, for training and preparedness exercises.

Reliability

  • Alerts send over wifi or mobile data, whichever the phone has available at the time.

  • Notifications arrive as a standard phone notification, including when the phone is locked, provided notifications are enabled for the app.

  • Software is software-only. No pagers, badges, or extra hardware to buy, issue, or replace.

Languages

  • Each staff member sets the language their own guidance and interface appear in.

  • This is set per person, not per property, so a mixed-language team each reads instructions in the language they understand fastest.

What software can't fix

Redcall runs on phones, and phones have limits. Being upfront about them is part of using the app safely.

  • Dead zones. If a phone has no signal and no wifi, it can't send or receive an alert. Redcall can't work around a dead spot in a building.

  • A phone that isn't on the person. If a phone is in a locker, charging in another room, or left at a desk, the person carrying it won't see the alert.

  • Silent or do not disturb. Notifications follow the phone's own sound settings. If sound is off, Redcall can't override it.

  • A phone that's off or flat. No power means no notification, the same as any app.

These are limits of any phone-based system, not something unique to Redcall. Agree with your team on where phones are carried during a shift, and treat Redcall as one part of your emergency plan, not the whole plan.
