The problem with relying on shouting down a hallway

In a boutique hotel or aged care facility, incidents rarely happen in front of a group. A single staff member on a night shift, a front-desk worker dealing with an aggressive guest, or a support worker alone in a resident's room has no fast way to bring in help or let anyone else know what's happening.

Phone calls take time to make and answer. Radios aren't always carried or switched on. By the time other staff find out, the situation has often already moved on without them, and the manager is the last to know.

How Redcall works

Step 1

Trigger

A staff member opens the app on their own phone, taps the alert button, and selects what's happening from a short list of situations.

Step 2

Everyone responds

On-site staff are notified immediately, see guidance written for that specific situation, and mark themselves safe or not safe.

Step 3

Manager coordinates

A manager sees who has responded, follows up directly with anyone who hasn't, and closes the alert once the situation is resolved.

What Redcall is, and isn't

Redcall is an awareness and coordination tool. It helps staff notice an incident faster, follow guidance written for that situation, and confirm who is safe.

It is not a personal panic button, and it is not a tactical responder. Emergency response stays with your staff and with emergency services at all times. In a life-threatening emergency, call 000 first. Redcall does not guarantee anyone's safety, and it is designed to support your existing procedures, not replace them.
